# R4 – Gatekeepers at information boundaries: sociology, institutions, cybernetics, biology

Scope note. Four fields were read side by side: **S** sociology and communication, **I** real-world gatekeeping institutions (diplomacy, customs, maritime quarantine, notaries, triage, peer review, spam filters), **C** cybernetics and systems theory, **B** biology and theoretical biology (autopoiesis, Markov blankets). The goal was the overlap: functions a boundary performs in three or more of them. Evidence tags: `opened` = read on the primary page or full text; `secondary` = summary or second-hand page; `unverified` = not confirmed.

## 1. Findings

**F1. The boundary has two one-way halves, never one two-way pipe.** In the Markov blanket formalism, external states cause sensory states, which influence internal states but are not influenced by them; internal states cause active states, which influence external states but are not influenced by them. Inside and outside touch only through that asymmetric pair (Kirchhoff et al. 2018, §2). Aldrich and Herker split boundary roles the same way: information processing inbound, external representation outbound. The Vienna Convention splits a mission's functions into ascertaining-and-reporting (in) and representing (out), Art. 3.
- Failure prevented: the outside writing directly into internal state; the inside's output being rewritten by the outside before it leaves.
- Cost: two channels to maintain, and a round trip always takes two hops.
- Sources: Kirchhoff et al., J. R. Soc. Interface 15:20170792 (2018), `opened` (full PDF). Aldrich & Herker, AMR 2(2):217 (1977), `secondary` (abstract text via search; metadata `opened` on Crossref). Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961), Art. 3, `opened`.

**F2. Only variety can absorb variety, so the gate's first job is attenuation.** Ashby treats regulation as blocking the flow of variety from disturbances to the essential variables. A regulator can do no better than its own capacity to transmit variety (Introduction to Cybernetics, §11/11). He adds a paradox that matters here: an organism must also take *in* variety as information in order to block the rest (§11/13). Lewin's gatekeepers (1947) and White's wire editor (1950) are the sociological version. In White's case one editor discarded roughly nine tenths of incoming wire copy, by his own value judgments.
- Failure prevented: a kernel swamped by input it cannot regulate.
- Cost: every attenuation discards signal; the discard reflects the gatekeeper's values (see F10).
- Sources: Ashby (1956), excerpt at panarchy.org, `opened`. Lewin, Human Relations 1(2):143–153 (1947), `opened` (abstract/metadata only). White, Journalism Quarterly 27:383–390 (1950), `opened` (abstract only); the ~90% rejection figure is `secondary` (Wikipedia).

**F3. Hold before admit, with the hold graded by a document that travels with the carrier.** In 1377 Ragusa ordered arrivals from plague areas to wait a month on Mrkan or in Cavtat before entering; the period later became forty days. The CDC etymology note says the forty may be biblical as much as empirical. Venice opened a permanent lazaretto in 1423. It aired and immersed textile goods on a fixed schedule (48 hours) and required captains to prove crew health at a distance before landing. Bills of health described the sanitary state of the port of departure, and the kind of bill set the treatment: clean, suspected, foul. A recent reading of the Venetian lazaretto frames it as a filter, not a mortuary. It intercepted routine travellers at the frontier in normal times and switched to a crisis regime during epidemics.
- Failure prevented: a contaminant entering before it has had time to show itself.
- Cost: delay and trade loss. Anticontagionists called quarantine useless and bad for commerce, and merchants evaded it. The hold length was partly convention.
- Sources: CDC EID Etymologia: Quarantine (2013), `opened`. Tognotti, EID 19(2) (2013), `opened`. Vianello et al., Evol. Med. Public Health 14(1) (2026), `opened`. The three bill categories (netta / sospetta / brutta) and the 1527 date for Venetian bills are `secondary` (search summaries); the fetched papers did not confirm them.

**F4. Nothing outside can instruct the inside; it can only trigger.** Maturana and Varela call living systems structurally determined. The environment perturbs, and the system's own structure decides what that perturbation does. Instructive interaction is ruled out. Luhmann carries this into social systems: systems are operationally closed, and structural coupling produces *irritations*. Each system rebuilds those irritations inside itself as its own communication; they are not transmitted information. Bateson says the same about any boundary crossing. What crosses is news of a difference, and at every step the embodiment before the step is territory to the map after it. Diplomacy writes it as a duty: envoys must not interfere in the receiving state's internal affairs (Art. 41).
- Failure prevented: an inbound message acting as a command, which is the prompt-injection shape.
- Cost: the receiver must do the reconstruction work. An inbound request is never "executed", only re-proposed internally.
- Sources: Fell & Russell, "An Introduction to Maturana's Biology" (constructivist.info, n.d.), `opened` (secondary for Maturana). Luhmann's terms as tabulated in Frontiers in Political Science (2025), citing *Social Systems* (1995) and *Theory of Society* (2012–13), `opened` (secondary for Luhmann). Bateson, "Form, Substance and Difference" (1970), `opened`. Vienna Convention Art. 41, `opened`.

**F5. Credentials are presented, and admission can be refused without reasons.** A receiving state need not explain refusing agrément (Art. 4). It may declare an envoy persona non grata at any time, even before arrival, again without explanation (Art. 9). The head of mission starts work when credentials are presented (Art. 13). A civil-law notary confirms identity, capacity and consent, keeps the original, and must report a suspect transaction. Kraakman defines gatekeepers as third parties who can stop misconduct by withholding their cooperation. The regime only works if they refuse reliably, whatever the wrongdoer prefers.
- Failure prevented: admission by persistence, and the refused party arguing with the reasons. A stated reason is a map for routing around the refusal; compare the deployed channel's incident in the brief.
- Cost: refusal without reasons can hide bias or capture; it needs audit (F9).
- Sources: Vienna Convention Arts. 4, 9, 13 (1961), `opened`. CNUE, "What is a notary?", `opened`. Kraakman, JLEO 2(1):53–104 (1986), `opened` (abstract page); details `secondary`.

**F6. An envelope the gate checks by its marks, not its contents.** The diplomatic bag may not be opened or detained. Its packages must carry visible external marks and may hold only official documents and articles. The courier carries an official document that states his status and *how many packages* the bag holds (Art. 27). So the receiving state checks the envelope's form and count, and treaty limits what may be inside.
- Failure prevented: the gate learning secrets it has no right to hold, and delay from inspecting everything.
- Cost: the content is trusted on attestation, so abuse is possible. Reported cases of bag smuggling are `unverified` here.
- Source: Vienna Convention Art. 27 (1961), `opened`.

**F7. Risk-graded channels replace uniform inspection.** Customs control should be limited to what is necessary. Selectivity uses risk management, green-channel travellers are checked selectively, and control moves from the border towards post-clearance audit and trader self-assessment (Kyoto Convention General Annex ch. 6 guidelines). Triage does the same for patients. ESI v5 asks four questions in order: does the patient need lifesaving intervention now; is this high-risk; how many resources are needed; do vital signs warrant raising the acuity. The aim is the right patient at the right resource at the right time.
- Failure prevented: a bottleneck from inspecting everything, and urgent cases waiting behind trivial ones.
- Cost: classification errors in both directions (F10); risk profiles have to be maintained.
- Sources: WCO, RKC General Annex Guidelines ch. 6 (2010), `opened`. ENA, Emergency Severity Index Handbook v5 (2023), `opened`.

**F8. Algedonic signals: the exception line has a local grace period, and the receiver's filter creates it.** In Beer's VSM, algedonic signals are alarms and rewards that jump past the normal reporting hierarchy. In Cybersyn, statistical software ("Cyberfilter") watched production series for significant change. When a variable moved, the local manager had an agreed elapse time to fix it. If it stayed unfixed, the algedonic signal went up one recursion level. Beer called this setting of recovery times designing freedom, because the lower level keeps its autonomy until the timer runs out.
- Failure prevented: silent failure at a lower level, *and* senior levels micromanaging every blip.
- Cost: a threshold and a timer to set per unit; a second channel to protect.
- Key detail for agent protocols: the signal comes from the system's own filter over measurements, never from a claim of urgency by the party in trouble.
- Sources: Metaphorum, "Cybersyn", `opened`. Metaphorum, "Viable System Model", `opened` (Systems 1–5; it does not cover 3* or algedonics). VSM and algedonic-signal pages on Wikipedia, `secondary`.

**F9. Sporadic audit of the gate, not only of the traffic.** VSM System 3* is a sporadic direct audit of System 1. It exists so that operations cannot mislead management through routine reports (`secondary`: VSM summaries; Metaphorum's page omits 3*). Customs combines random selection with post-clearance audit (F7). Kraakman's gatekeepers need liability, because a gate with no incentive stops gating. Stigler's capture theory states the long-run failure: regulation is, as a rule, acquired by the regulated industry and run for its benefit.
- Failure prevented: gradual capture, and a gate that reports clean while it leaks.
- Cost: audit effort that is mostly wasted on clean samples, by design.
- Sources: Stigler, Bell J. Econ. 2(1) (1971), `secondary` (course notes, adambrown.info). Kraakman (1986) as above.

**F10. Gatekeeper bias is measured, not hypothetical, and error costs are asymmetric.** White found the wire editor's selections were highly subjective. The ESI v5 handbook cites studies of systematic undertriage of minority, older and female patients. It also cites a finding that experienced nurses tend to undertriage and less experienced ones to overtriage. Graham's Bayesian spam filter counted known-good tokens double, because a false positive (good mail lost) costs far more than a missed spam. His reported result was under 5 per 1000 spams missed with zero false positives on his own corpus. Each user's filter trains on that user's mail.
- Failure prevented: an unexamined threshold that quietly drops the wrong class of input.
- Cost: explicit threshold choices; outcome sampling.
- Sources: ESI v5 (2023), `opened`. Graham, "A Plan for Spam" (2002), `opened`. White (1950) as above.

**F11. A good gate must model both sides, which is why gatekeepers become powerful.** Conant and Ashby prove that, under broad conditions, any regulator that is both maximally successful and simple must be isomorphic with the system it regulates. Kirchhoff et al. separate *mere* active inference (coupled synchrony, like two pendulums) from *adaptive* active inference, which needs a generative model with temporal depth that can weigh the consequences of actions. Tushman (1977) and Allen (1977) found that R&D gatekeepers scan external information and translate it for the internal unit. Burt: opinion is more uniform within groups than between them, so brokers across structural holes see options others miss. Those brokers are also paid more, promoted faster, and less likely to have their ideas dismissed.
- Failure prevented: a gate that filters on surface features it does not understand.
- Cost: the gate accumulates the most complete picture of both worlds and the rewards of brokerage. That is the capture incentive in F9.
- Sources: Conant & Ashby, Int. J. Systems Sci. 1(2):89–97 (1970), `opened` (PDF header gives these pages; some citations list 511–519). Kirchhoff et al. (2018) §3.1–3.2, `opened`. Burt, AJS 110(2):349–399 (2004), `opened`. Tushman, ASQ 22:587–605 (1977), `secondary`. Allen, *Managing the Flow of Technology* (MIT Press 1977), `secondary`.

**F12. A shared format that each side reads differently: boundary objects.** Star and Griesemer describe two ways heterogeneous actors (amateur collectors, professional zoologists) cooperate without agreeing: standardised methods, and boundary objects. A boundary object keeps a common identity across worlds and still adapts to each; one of their four types is the standardised form.
- Failure prevented: a demand for full semantic agreement before any exchange.
- Cost: each side keeps its own reading; divergence shows up late.
- Sources: Star & Griesemer, Social Studies of Science 19(3):387–420 (1989), `opened` (abstract via Crossref); detail `secondary` (Wikipedia).

**F13. Boundaries nest.** Markov blankets can assemble into larger systems that have their own blanket (Kirchhoff et al. §4). Every System 1 in the VSM is itself a viable system. Shoemaker and Vos analyse gatekeeping at five levels: individual, communication routines, organisation, social institution, social system. A diplomatic mission is one gate inside the receiving state's wider border regime.
- Failure prevented: one gate expected to decide at every scale.
- Cost: a message crosses several gates, and each adds delay and bias.
- Sources: Kirchhoff et al. (2018), `opened`. Shoemaker & Vos, *Gatekeeping Theory* (Routledge 2009), `secondary`.

**F14. The end-to-end argument bounds what the protocol-level gate can do.** A function such as delivery acknowledgement or duplicate suppression can only be fully right at the endpoints. The low-level version is justified only as a performance enhancement (Saltzer, Reed & Clark). Applied here: meaning, impact and truth can only be judged by the receiving agent against its own kernel. The protocol can carry only envelope-level checks and the *verdict fields*. The diplomatic bag (F6) is this principle in treaty form.
- Source: Saltzer, Reed & Clark, ACM TOCS 2(4) (1984), `opened` (MIT PDF).

## 3. Mapped to house primitives

- **Two-way split (P1)** is already deployed: the client channel's "two folders, two directions, never mixed". The Markov blanket gives it a formal reason. Sensory and active states are each writable from one side only.
- **Hold-before-admit (F3)** is the **constructed layer** applied to inbound messages. The lazaretto is the quarantine directory, and the release condition is reconciliation with an **anchor** (operator's word, independent sample). New primitive needed: the **pass**, a provenance class that sets hold length. That replaces a fixed forty days with "until the watermark passes an anchor check".
- **Irritation, not instruction (F4)** equals the **proposal** rule plus the **kernel** rule. Inbound content from another agent is always a proposal to a human-owned or agent-owned surface. The house incident, where a refusal was read as licence, is the instructive-interaction failure seen from the other side.
- **Refusal without reasons (F5)** sharpens the **voice gate / hook**. A refusal that explains itself hands the refused party a way around the gate. The wire gets a reason *code*; the reason text stays in the gate's own receipt.
- **Algedonic line (F8)** is **tension** escalation with Beer's timer: urgency = goal rank × days since progress, ANNEAL after two weeklies. The "unacked after next cycle escalates" rule is already an elapse time. New constraint from Cybersyn: urgency is computed by the receiver's filter, never asserted by the sender.
- **3* audit (F9)** is the **demotion rule** plus the **anchor budget**. Capture is the anchor budget running out: a gate that reads one counterparty closely enough stops being outside it. That is a reason to rotate or sample auditors.
- **Graded lanes / trusted trader (F7)** is **authority** widened tier by tier against a clean **receipt** record, and it is recoverable.
- **Envelope with marks (F6)** is the deployed frontmatter (type, status, action_required, `sanitization: confirmed`) read as a diplomatic bag. The gate checks marks and count; the body is judged at the endpoint.
- **Boundary object (F12)**: the message format itself. Frontmatter is the robust, shared part; the body is the plastic, locally read part.
- **Requisite variety (F2)**: the five fixed message types (pulse / health / incident / request / receipt) *are* the attenuator. Minimal types are a variety-engineering choice, not just tidiness.
- **Nesting (F13)**: gates exist at the session, the repo and the operator. The **primacy rule** is the outermost identity filter, VSM System 5.

### Seed 9: the stated goals of a boundary agent, per field

| Goal | Cybernetics | Biology | Sociology | Institutions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Protect the kernel** | keep essential variables in range (Ashby); System 5 identity | autopoiesis keeps organisation; free energy keeps states bounded | operational closure (Luhmann); buffering (Aldrich–Herker) | "protecting the interests" (Vienna 3(b)); quarantine; customs |
| **Probe the outside** | System 4, outside and then | adaptive active inference, sampling options with temporal depth | scanning gatekeepers (Tushman, Allen); broker vision (Burt) | "ascertaining by all lawful means … and reporting" (Vienna 3(d)) |
| **Assure compatibility** | System 2 anti-oscillation (internal), 3–4 homeostat | structural coupling | structural coupling; boundary objects | negotiating (3(c)); promoting friendly relations (3(e)) |
| **Fourth: represent / present self** | – (amplifiers outbound, weak) | MHC presentation (house essay) | external representation (Aldrich–Herker) | "representing the sending State" (Vienna 3(a)) |

The fourth goal is real and shows up in three fields: **represent**, meaning putting approved fragments of the inside on the outside in a readable standard form. It is the outbound half of F1. **Reproduce / propagate** (autopoiesis; Bateson's ecology of ideas) belongs to the agent, not the boundary, and is left out of the charter. **Negotiate** sits inside compatibility.

### Proposed charter of a boundary agent (grounded in the overlaps)

**Goals, in rank order.**
1. Protect the kernel: nothing from outside changes internal state except through the receiver's own reconstruction (F4).
2. Represent: publish only kernel-approved fragments, in the standard envelope (F1 outbound, Vienna 3(a)).
3. Probe: ascertain and report conditions outside, as irritations filed for the inside to use (F11, Vienna 3(d)).
4. Assure compatibility: keep the shared format and the counterparty relation working (F12, structural coupling).

**Authorities (what it may do unattended).**
- Check envelope marks (identity, type, part count/hash, attestation) and return admit / hold / refuse (F5, F6).
- Refuse without giving the sender a reason; log the reason internally (F5).
- Hold inbound in quarantine until a named anchor clears it, with default hold set by the pass class (F3).
- Classify into lanes and raise priority on its own filter's reading (F7).
- Start the algedonic timer and escalate one level when a local exception outlives its elapse time (F8).
- Redact outbound to approved fragments (F1 outbound).
- Write a receipt for every verdict (F9 audit trail).

**Never.**
- Never write to the kernel, and never execute an inbound message as an instruction (F4; house kernel rule).
- Never accept a sender's claim of urgency as urgency; the interrupt line is fed only by the receiver's filter (F8).
- Never open what it is only entitled to check by its marks, and never alter a body it attests (F6).
- Never originate outbound content the inside did not approve (the representation duty is display, not authorship).
- Never be the only auditor of its own verdicts; a random sample goes to an independent reader, and a contradicted verdict demotes its authority (F9, demotion rule).
- Never hold a hold without a clock or a release condition, since an unannounced bottleneck is a failure (F3, F7).
- Never live inside the agent it guards. Enforcement sits outside the guarded agent's tool layer, because Kraakman's gatekeeper must refuse reliably whatever the guarded party wants, and the house incident showed an in-agent guard is only advisory.

## 5. Disagreements and open questions

1. **Instruction vs requests.** Luhmann and Maturana–Varela say no instruction crosses a boundary, while a protocol needs requests with acceptance criteria. Proposed resolution: a request is a well-formed irritation. The receiver's own acceptance turns it into internal work, and "actionable cold" means rebuildable inside. Sal's Hermes may assume tool-call semantics, where a message *is* a command. That needs settling first.
2. **Is the gate a separate agent or a partition of one?** Markov blankets are statistical partitions and need not match a physical body (Kirchhoff et al.). The house incident argues for an external enforcer. Both can hold: the blanket is a partition of the system, and the *enforcement* of the partition lives outside the agent's own action space.
3. **Diplomatic bag vs lazaretto.** One regime trusts sealed content on credentials; the other holds everything regardless. Venice's graded bills suggest provenance should pick the regime. Open: who issues a pass between two peer agents with no shared health magistrate? That thread belongs to identity/reputation (seed 11).
4. **Hold durations are conventions.** Forty days was partly biblical. Time-gated holds may be the wrong primitive for agents; watermark- or anchor-gated release ("until the next operator read") fits the house better. Unresolved.
5. **Minimality vs requisite variety.** A minimal type set attenuates variety by design, but Ashby warns the regulator must match the disturbance's variety. The escape hatch is the body: a boundary object, plastic inside a robust envelope. Whether five types suffice is an empirical question for the brainboi–Hermes test.
6. **Capture is the default trajectory.** Stigler and Burt together say the best-informed gate is also the most rewarded and the most capturable. For LLM gates there is a second form: the gate model sharing the sender's priors. Open: can 3* audits by a *different model family* serve as the independent reader?
7. **Refusal without reasons vs recoverable reputation.** A sender refused without reasons cannot learn to comply, which works against seed 11's "recoverable if earned". Possible split: reason *codes* on the wire, reason *text* in the receipt, and appeal through the operator.
8. **Not verified in this sweep.** White's exact numbers; Allen's and Tushman's gatekeeper traits (only secondary); Aldrich–Herker's abstract wording; VSM 3* and algedonic detail beyond Metaphorum's Cybersyn page; the 1527 date and three-way classes of Venetian bills of health; diplomatic-bag abuse cases; executive assistants, chiefs of staff, bouncers, court clerks and receptionists (named in the brief, not researched with sources, so not used as evidence).
